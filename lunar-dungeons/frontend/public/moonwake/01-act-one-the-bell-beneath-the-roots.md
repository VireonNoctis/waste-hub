# Act I — The Bell Beneath the Roots
## The Mosslight Wilds
### A Lantern Without a Flame

**The first note: Return.**
**The first guardian: The Verdant Colossus.**
**The movement art awakened: Moon Dash.**

## Chapter 1 — The observatory wakes

The broken dome held a small circle of sky.
It was enough sky for the moon to be seen.
It was not enough sky for the old astronomers to have finished their work.
Their brass instruments leaned toward the opening as if they were still waiting for a star.
Silver dust lay over every table.
A chart had been left open beneath a glass weight.
The chart showed a city that no longer appeared on any map.
Iri stood beside the chart with her wings folded close.
The Wayfarer watched the lantern glow in her hands.
Its glass was clear.
Its wick was pale.
There was no flame inside it.
There was a sound like a small room breathing.
"Does it belong to you?" the Wayfarer asked.
Iri looked at the lantern.
"I do not know."
"Then why are you holding it?"
"Because it was waiting."
The Wayfarer looked toward the doorway.
Outside, pale roots had grown across the threshold.
They did not block the passage.
They had grown around the stone as though holding it open.
Iri followed the Wayfarer's gaze.
"Mosslight," she said.
"Is it safe?"
"Safe is a word people use when they have not yet met the forest."
"That is not an answer."
"No," said Iri.
"It is a warning to ask a better question."
The Wayfarer asked what the forest wanted.
Iri said that forests did not want in the way cities wanted.
They made room.
They closed wounds.
They remembered rain.
They did not promise that every road would remain easy.
A moth landed on the lantern.
The glass brightened.
For a moment the Wayfarer heard a voice speaking from very far away.
It sounded like someone calling a person home.
Then the sound became the rustle of leaves.
Iri turned the lantern toward the open gate.
"Come," she said.
"We should let the road choose whether it recognises us."

### The first threshold

The observatory steps descended beneath a canopy of white leaves.
Every leaf held a thin line of silver along its edge.
The morning did not arrive in Mosslight.
Instead, a green-gold dusk rested between the branches.
Small lights moved through the roots.
Some were fireflies.
Some were lantern-moths.
Some were memories too small to have names.
The Wayfarer stepped onto the first bridge.
A root bent beneath their weight.
It did not break.
It rose again when they passed.
The bridge led to a stone arch.
The arch bore five empty circles.
Iri touched the smallest circle.
"Someone has tried to scratch the mark away."
"Who?"
"Perhaps someone who believed a mark could be stolen."
"Can it?"
"Not if it was freely given."
The Wayfarer examined the circles.
The first held the outline of a path.
The second held a little hearth.
The third held a blank nameplate.
The fourth held two stars at a measured distance.
The fifth held an open eye.
The first circle was warm.
The others were cold.
Iri withdrew her hand.
"Return," she whispered.
The root beneath the arch moved.
A passage opened through the leaves.
The journey entered the forest.

## Chapter 2 — Bell Garden

### The garden that listened

Bell Garden was not a garden planted by one hand.
It was a hundred years of small decisions growing together.
A family had left a window open.
A traveller had scattered seeds.
A child had buried a broken bell to keep it from being thrown away.
An old tree had leaned toward the sound.
The roots had built the rest.
Flowers shaped like little bells hung from the branches.
They rang only when the wind carried a remembered name.
Some flowers rang clearly.
Some gave only a small tremor.
One flower did not ring at all.
The Wayfarer stopped beneath it.
Iri looked up.
"That one has lost its voice."
"Can it be repaired?"
"Perhaps."
The Wayfarer reached toward the flower.
Iri caught their wrist.
"Do not ask it to speak."
"Why?"
"Because it may not wish to."
The Wayfarer lowered their hand.
The flower opened.
A moth slipped out.
Its wings were nearly transparent.
It circled the lantern and disappeared into the leaves.
A path of pale petals appeared on the ground.
It led to a stone bench.
The bench bore two shallow places where someone had once sat.
Beside it lay a child's wooden whistle.
The whistle was cracked.
The Wayfarer picked it up.
A note sounded without breath.
The note was not a melody.
It was the shape of a question.
Iri closed her eyes.
"I know this," she said.
"What is it?"
"A warning."
"From whom?"
"I cannot remember."
She placed the whistle beside the bench.
The note faded.
The roots shifted.
A small creature emerged from the soil.
It had a moss-covered back and a face like a seed.
It circled the Wayfarer twice.
Then it sprang toward the lantern.
The Wayfarer stepped aside.
The creature landed on a patch of soft earth.
It stared at them.
It did not look hungry.
It looked startled.
Iri crouched.
"Hello, little one."
The creature lowered its head.
A pale line appeared along its back.
It was the mark of the Bell Garden.
"Do not strike first," Iri said.
"How will I know if it is dangerous?"
"Watch what it protects."
The creature moved between them and the buried bell.
The bell was hidden beneath a wall of roots.
The mossling had not been attacking the Wayfarer.
It had been asking them to stay away.
The Wayfarer stepped back.
The creature relaxed.
Iri smiled faintly.
"That is the first thing Mosslight teaches."
"What?"
"Not every obstacle is an enemy."

### The bell-keeper's little promise

An old gardener sat beneath the largest tree.
His name was Pell.
He had forgotten the name of the person who taught him to prune the bell flowers.
He remembered the hands.
He remembered the patience.
He remembered that the lesson had happened on a rainy afternoon.
He kept a small notebook tied to his wrist.
Every page contained a drawing of the same tree.
The drawings changed.
One showed a young sapling.
One showed a tree bending beneath snow.
One showed a hollow where a bird had nested.
One showed the roots extending beneath the town.
Pell did not write the missing name.
He had promised not to write it until the person chose to return.
"Do you know who I am?" Pell asked.
Iri shook her head.
"Not by name."
"Good."
The Wayfarer looked at the notebook.
"Why is that good?"
"Because if you knew my name, you might think you knew my story."
Pell closed the book.
"Names are useful."
"They are not the whole of anyone."
He pointed toward the buried bell.
"That bell is not a door."
"What is it?"
"A sentence someone could not finish."
The Wayfarer asked who buried it.
Pell looked at Iri.
Iri looked at the roots.
"Someone who wanted the warning to survive," Pell said.
"The forest has been keeping it for a long time."
"Can we hear it?"
"Only if you do not ring it for your own sake."
The Wayfarer asked how they could ring it without wanting to hear it.
Pell smiled.
"You can listen for the sake of the people who could not finish speaking."
He opened the notebook again.
On the last page was a small drawing of the lantern.
Beneath it was a blank space.
Pell turned the page before the Wayfarer could ask.
The garden continued to listen.

### Iri's first thread

The Wayfarer found Iri beside a shallow pool.
The water reflected the lantern but not the faces above it.
Iri held one hand under the surface.
A tiny moth rested on her finger.
"Do you come here often?" the Wayfarer asked.
"I used to."
"Before what?"
"Before I learned to forget the beginning of a sentence."
The Wayfarer sat beside her.
The pool remained still.
Iri said that she had been an archivist in Moonwake.
She had recorded the names of streets, animals, ceremonies, and every person who asked to be remembered.
She had believed that careful records could protect people.
She had believed that the more she wrote, the less anyone would be lost.
Then she had discovered that a name could become a lock.
Someone could read a record and decide that the person inside it was no longer allowed to change.
Someone could point to an old promise and demand that it be kept forever.
Someone could use a memory as proof that a person belonged to them.
Iri looked at the lantern.
"I wanted to protect the archive."
"What did you do?"
"I removed my own name."
"Why?"
"Because the Quiet had begun to follow names through the shelves."
"Did it work?"
"For a while."
"What happened to you?"
"I became someone who could carry a lantern but could not remember why she had chosen it."
The Wayfarer listened.
They did not ask Iri to prove the story.
They did not ask whether she regretted it.
They let the pool hold the silence.
After a while, Iri said, "Thank you."
"For what?"
"For not trying to finish my sentence."

### Quest — The Bell Kept Quiet

The Bell Garden's warning had been divided into three fragments.
One rested beneath the old bench.
One was held in a flower that never rang.
One lay in the roots around the buried bell.
The first fragment sounded like rain against a roof.
The second sounded like a voice calling from another room.
The third sounded like a hand resting on a door.
The Wayfarer gathered the fragments without forcing the flower to ring.
They carried them to the root wall.
Pell waited there.
Iri stood beside him.
The Wayfarer offered the fragments.
Pell did not take them.
"Let the roots finish their warning," he said.
The Wayfarer placed the fragments in the earth.
The roots drew them together.
The buried bell answered once.
The sound travelled through every leaf.
No creature fled.
No branch broke.
The mossling lowered its head.
The flower that had lost its voice trembled.
The warning became words.
"Do not make a monument of what must remain alive."
Pell closed his notebook.
He had heard the sentence before.
He had never heard it completed.
He began to cry, but not from sadness alone.
The missing name did not return.
Instead, he remembered the lesson.
He remembered the person who had taught it.
He remembered that the person had asked to be remembered as someone who had changed.
Pell did not say the name.
He left the page blank.
The roots accepted the fragments.
The Wayfarer received the first mark of Return.
The mark was not a key.
It was a small invitation to keep a path open.
Iri looked at the lantern.
Its glass brightened.
A line of green light crossed the wick.
"One note," she said.
"One place that remembers how to welcome."
The Wayfarer looked toward the deeper forest.
The path was still there.
It had not been made easier.
It had been made possible.

## Chapter 3 — The Root Archive

### The library that grew

The Root Archive stood inside a hollow tree.
Its shelves were living branches.
Its books were leaves folded into quiet shapes.
Its catalogues were written in rings of wood.
The oldest records had no titles.
They had small knots where a person had once touched the bark.
A thorn sprite hovered near the entrance.
It flashed once.
Then it dove toward the lantern.
The Wayfarer moved aside.
The sprite returned to its branch.
It flashed again.
Iri watched carefully.
"It is guarding the upper shelves."
"From us?"
"From anyone who would take without asking."
The Wayfarer left the lantern on the stone floor.
The sprite settled.
A shelf unfolded.
Inside lay a book made from pale bark.
Its pages were blank.
The Wayfarer opened it.
A picture appeared.
It showed two people carrying a table through rain.
One person was laughing.
The other was trying not to drop a bowl.
The picture faded.
A second appeared.
It showed the same table beside a window.
There were three cups.
A fourth place had been left empty.
The Wayfarer asked Iri why the book showed no names.
"Because its owners did not ask for them to be recorded."
"Then how can it remember?"
"By remembering what they chose to leave."
The Wayfarer closed the book.
The shelf folded around it.
A soft green light ran through the branch.
The archive had recognised a respectful guest.

### The thorn sprite's lesson

The thorn sprite was not a monster.
It was a question that had grown wings.
It flashed whenever someone approached the shelves too quickly.
It dove only when a hand reached for a book without permission.
Its thorns were tiny and pale.
They had never been used to kill.
They had been used to keep pages from being torn.
The Wayfarer followed it along a narrow branch.
The sprite stopped above a gap.
Below the gap was a room full of fallen leaves.
Each leaf carried one sentence.
Some sentences were complete.
Some ended halfway through.
One said, "I wanted to tell you..."
Another said, "The door is still..."
Another said, "I forgive..."
The Wayfarer reached for the incomplete leaf.
Iri shook her head.
"Let it be."
"What if someone needs to finish it?"
"Then the person who owns the sentence may return."
The Wayfarer let the leaf remain.
The sprite circled them once.
It landed on the lantern.
For the first time, its light did not flash.
It glowed softly.
A hidden stair opened in the tree.
The Wayfarer descended.
At the bottom was a reliquary.
Inside rested the first secret mark.

### Secret — The Mosslight Reliquary

The reliquary held a seed of glass.
It was clear on one side and green on the other.
When the Wayfarer touched it, a memory appeared.
Auralis stood in the observatory garden.
She was younger.
Her wings were bright.
She was planting a seed beside the door.
A voice asked what it would become.
Auralis answered, "A path."
The voice asked whether paths grew from seeds.
Auralis said, "Only when someone walks beside them."
The memory changed.
The seed was buried beneath the roots.
Auralis placed the lantern beside it.
She spoke a name.
The sound was too faint to understand.
Then she said, "Do not let the door become a wall."
The memory ended.
The Wayfarer kept the fragment.
It was the first of five hidden marks.
The fragment did not unlock the Underlight by itself.
It changed the lantern's reflection.
A faint silver line appeared around the glass.

### The root archive's final room

At the deepest shelf, the Wayfarer found a book with no spine.
It was tied with a thread of living green.
Iri refused to touch it.
The Wayfarer asked why.
"Because I remember opening it."
"What happened?"
"I do not know."
"Then we can open it together."
Iri stared at the book.
The lantern warmed between them.
She did not move.
The Wayfarer waited.
The archive creaked.
A branch lowered itself like a hand.
Iri finally touched the thread.
The book opened.
A page showed a drawing of Moonwake before it disappeared.
The observatory stood bright beneath a full moon.
The streets were full of people.
The archive doors were open.
Auralis stood in the doorway with the lantern.
She was smiling.
Iri drew a breath.
"That is me."
The Wayfarer nodded.
"Yes."
"I thought I had been a stranger to this place."
"You were not."
"I cannot remember being happy."
"You do not have to remember everything at once."
The page turned by itself.
The next page was blank.
Iri closed the book.
She did not ask the archive to fill it.
The root accepted the silence.
The lantern glowed more strongly.
A new passage appeared toward Briar Well.

## Chapter 4 — Briar Well

### The colossus beneath the garden

Briar Well was a circular chamber beneath the oldest roots.
Its walls were made from twisted wood and pale stone.
The floor was covered in soft moss.
At the centre stood a great figure.
It was built from branches, roots, and a broken bell.
Its shoulders held small trees.
Its hands rested on the ground.
Its face had no mouth.
A line of green light crossed its chest.
The Verdant Colossus was awake.
The Wayfarer approached.
The Colossus raised one hand.
Roots moved across the floor.
They formed a wide ring.
The Wayfarer stopped.
Iri placed the lantern on the ground.
The Colossus lowered its hand.
For a moment, the chamber was quiet.
Then the Colossus pointed to the wall.
A mural showed the five wardens.
Each carried a note of light.
Orin stood at the centre.
Auralis stood beside them.
The mural had been damaged.
Someone had scratched away the face of the Keeper.
The Colossus touched the scratch.
A leaf grew over it.
The Wayfarer understood.
The guardian was not defending a throne.
It was defending the last record of a promise.
A bell sounded beneath the floor.
The Colossus stepped forward.
Its arms rose.
Roots began to move.
The fight began.

### The shape of the encounter

The Colossus did not attack at random.
It raised both arms before the roots crossed the floor.
The moss beneath the Wayfarer brightened where the roots would emerge.
The safe places were small but clear.
The Colossus paused after each sweep.
The pause was not weakness.
It was an invitation to move.
The Wayfarer learned to wait for the ground to speak.
They learned to move without rushing.
They used the lantern strike only when the Colossus lowered its guard.
The first strike broke a branch.
The second revealed a bell-shaped knot.
The third made the green light flicker.
The Colossus stepped back.
A new pattern appeared.
Roots rose from the walls.
The floor became a living bridge.
The Wayfarer jumped across.
The Colossus struck the ground.
The bridge shook.
The Wayfarer held the lantern close.
Its light drew a path through the moving roots.
The guardian's chest opened.
Inside was a small note of green light.
The Wayfarer did not seize it.
They waited.
The Colossus lowered its hands.
The light moved toward the lantern by itself.
The battle ended.
No trophy fell.
No crown appeared.
The Colossus knelt.
The roots around the chamber relaxed.
Its face changed.
A small mouth appeared.
It spoke in a voice like rain.
"Return is not the act of bringing someone back."
"It is the act of leaving a way for them to choose."
The Wayfarer lowered the lantern.
The first note entered the glass.
The chamber filled with leaves.
The Colossus became still.
It had not died.
It had finished guarding the warning.
The mossling returned to the chamber.
It rested against the Colossus's hand.
The guardian closed its fingers gently around the creature.
A new mark appeared on the Wayfarer's palm.
It was a curve shaped like a path.
The moon dash awakened.
The ability was not a gift of speed alone.
It was the power to cross a short distance without losing the direction of home.

### The first movement art

The Wayfarer stood at the chamber's edge.
A wide crack divided the floor.
The new mark warmed.
They took a breath.
Then they moved.
For one brief instant, the world became a line of green light.
The gap passed beneath their feet.
They landed on the far side.
Iri laughed.
It was a small sound.
She had not laughed in a long time.
The Wayfarer looked back.
The Colossus had returned to its kneeling place.
The mossling slept beside it.
A root curled around the lantern.
The chamber had become a garden again.
Iri said, "The first art is called Moon Dash."
"Did you know it would happen?"
"No."
"Then why are you smiling?"
"Because it happened without anyone taking it from you."
The Wayfarer looked at the new mark.
It shone faintly.
The path beyond Briar Well opened.
A red light flickered on the horizon.
The next kingdom waited inside a caldera.
Iri looked toward it.
"Ember Hollow."
"What should we expect?"
"Warmth."
"That sounds better than a warning."
"It is."
She paused.
"Warmth can also be a burden when a city forgets how to rest."
The Wayfarer picked up the lantern.
The first act ended.
The forest did not close behind them.
It kept the way home open.

## Chapter 5 — After the bell

### Pell's final page

Before leaving Mosslight, the Wayfarer returned to Pell.
The old gardener was sitting beneath the bell flowers.
His notebook rested on his knees.
The page that had once been blank now held a small drawing.
It showed two hands planting a seed.
Neither hand had a name.
Pell smiled at the drawing.
"I remember the lesson now."
"Do you remember who taught it?"
"I remember what they asked me to remember."
"Is that enough?"
"It is enough for today."
Pell tore no page from the book.
He added a new leaf to the cover.
It had been pressed between two pieces of bark.
The leaf held a faint green note.
Pell gave it to the Wayfarer.
"Take this to the next kingdom."
"What is it?"
"A reminder that a hearth can be shared by strangers."
The Wayfarer thanked him.
Pell looked toward the forest gate.
"Tell Vey that the roots still remember his promise."
Iri nodded.
"I will."
Pell closed the notebook.
The old name remained unwritten.
The path remained open.

### Iri's second truth

At the edge of the forest, Iri stopped.
The lantern cast two shadows.
One belonged to her.
The other belonged to someone she could almost remember.
"I think I know why I erased my name," she said.
The Wayfarer waited.
"I believed that if the Quiet could not find me, it could not follow the others."
"Did it work?"
"It kept the archive safe for a time."
"Did you choose it?"
"Yes."
"Would you choose it again?"
Iri watched a moth pass through the light.
"I do not know."
The Wayfarer did not tell her what she should have done.
They asked what she wanted now.
"I want to learn the name without being made to wear it."
"Then we can leave it until you are ready."
Iri smiled.
"That is a better promise."
The lantern brightened.
The second shadow faded.
The Wayfarer and Iri stepped onto the road to Ember Hollow.

### Interlude — The old bell's dream

That night, the buried bell dreamed.
It dreamed of Moonwake before the Quiet.
It dreamed of a festival where the people brought unfinished stories.
A child spoke of a bird that had not yet learned to fly.
A baker spoke of a loaf that had collapsed and become better as a pudding.
A tired guard spoke of a road they no longer wished to patrol.
Auralis wrote none of these stories down.
She listened.
When the festival ended, the Lumen glowed like a small sunrise.
The bell asked the moon why.
The moon answered, "Because no one was asked to finish."
The bell asked what would happen when the stories were forgotten.
The moon said, "They will change."
The bell asked whether change was loss.
The moon said, "Sometimes."
Then it added, "Sometimes it is how a story survives."
The bell woke beneath the roots.
It no longer feared the sound of silence.
It had learned the difference between a pause and an ending.

## The Mosslight Chronicle — Recovered pages

### The Rootbound Gate

The gate was built for people who travelled slowly.
It had no lock.
Its hinges were made of living wood.
The first Keeper placed a small stone beneath it.
The stone was not a foundation.
It was a promise that the gate could be held open by anyone who wished to welcome another.
The Wayfarer's footsteps made the stone glow.
The gate recognised a guest.
It did not demand a name.

### Fernfall Crossing

Fernfall was a bridge made from overlapping leaves.
It bent toward the river.
It rose when the rain was heavy.
Travellers once tied ribbons to its branches.
Each ribbon marked a journey that had been completed.
The oldest ribbon had no colour.
It had been worn smooth by many hands.
It marked a journey someone had chosen not to describe.
The forest kept it anyway.

### The Sunken Grove

The Sunken Grove lay below a shallow lake.
The trees grew downward from the surface.
Their roots made a ceiling of green.
A person could walk beneath the water without becoming wet.
The grove remembered the sound of rain from before the first bell.
Some visitors heard their own footsteps.
Others heard the footsteps of people they had never met.
The grove did not explain the difference.

### Mosskeeper Halls

The halls were grown around the trunks of seven trees.
Each tree held a different kind of memory.
One held celebrations.
One held ordinary chores.
One held names freely given.
One held names deliberately released.
One held promises.
One held apologies.
The last held blank pages.
The Mosskeepers said that the blank tree was the most important.
It gave the others room to grow.

### Thornwake Tunnels

The tunnels were narrow.
The roots pressed close to the stone.
Lantern-moths lived in the cracks.
They made their nests from pieces of old maps.
The maps showed roads that had never existed.
A traveller who followed one would always reach a place where someone needed help.
The Mosskeepers did not consider this a useful map.
The moths considered it perfect.

### The Buried Orchard

The orchard held trees that bore no fruit.
Instead, each branch carried a small brass tag.
The tags listed things people had once asked to remember.
A first snowfall.
A warm cup.
A letter never sent.
A dog waiting at a gate.
The orchard was not a monument to the past.
It was a place where the living could leave small things without being asked to explain them.

### Wyrmroot Warrens

The warrens were made by an ancient root-creature.
It moved slowly beneath the soil.
It could hear every footstep above it.
It never emerged when people were afraid.
It surfaced only when a traveller sat down and waited.
The creature's back was covered in moss.
Small flowers grew between its scales.
The Mosskeepers called it the patient road.

### The Hollow Stump

The stump was the remnant of a tree that had sheltered the first refugees from the Quiet.
Its hollow was large enough for a whole family.
The inside was lined with soft bark.
A child's height marks remained on the wall.
The marks had not been updated for centuries.
They were left in place because the child had asked not to be measured again.

### Briarwatch Depths

The depths were built around a well of living roots.
The roots carried warnings from one part of the forest to another.
They did not carry private memories.
They carried requests for help.
A traveller could place a hand on the wall and feel the direction of a distant need.
The Wayfarer felt the Colossus waiting.
The forest had been trying to say so for many years.

### Lanternfen

Lanternfen was a low meadow filled with silver mist.
The water reflected stars that were not visible in the sky.
Each lantern-moth carried a tiny piece of the first note.
They gathered around people who had lost their way.
They never led anyone by force.
They moved toward the place where a path had been left open.

### The Verdant Maze

The maze rearranged itself whenever someone insisted on finding the quickest route.
It opened when a traveller admitted they were lost.
Its walls were made of roots with soft green light in their veins.
A person could hear companions through the walls.
The voices were not always nearby.
The maze taught that distance was not the same as abandonment.

### Oldgrowth Catacombs

The catacombs held the roots of trees that had died naturally.
Their names were not written.
Their rings recorded seasons, storms, and the pressure of other roots.
The Mosskeepers visited to thank them.
Nothing was buried there against its will.
The dead trees had made their own room for rest.

### The Spore Cathedral

The cathedral was not built for worship.
It was built to hold the echo of the first bell.
The spores glowed when someone spoke a memory with care.
They grew brighter when someone admitted that they did not know.
The cathedral had no altar.
It had a circle of seats.
Every seat was the same height.

### Heartwood Descent

The descent led toward Briar Well.
The roots grew thicker.
The floor became warmer.
A bell-shaped shadow moved below the stone.
The Wayfarer could hear the Colossus breathing.
It was not sleeping.
It was waiting for the warning to be heard.

### The Warden's Ring

The ring was the chamber where the Verdant Colossus stood.
It was shaped from the roots of the first tree.
A green note rested in the guardian's chest.
The note could not be taken.
It had to be invited.
When the Wayfarer listened, the Colossus lowered its hands.
The first note returned to the lantern.
The gate to Ember Hollow opened.

### The Ancient Canopy

The canopy was visible from every path in Mosslight.
Its highest branches held a fragment of the moon's reflection.
The leaves moved even when there was no wind.
The Mosskeepers said the canopy was listening to the next kingdom.
It knew the fire would need a place to rest.
It knew the ice would need a voice.
It knew the stars would need room.
It knew the Quiet would need to be heard.

## Dialogue folio — Pell, Iri, and the garden

**Pell:** "The tree does not remember every hand that touched it."
**Wayfarer:** "Then how does it remember?"
**Pell:** "By growing where those hands made room."
**Iri:** "I used to think an archive had to be complete."
**Pell:** "A complete archive is a room with no door."
**Wayfarer:** "What should we keep?"
**Pell:** "What someone chooses to leave."
**Iri:** "And what should we release?"
**Pell:** "What someone asks us to stop carrying."
**Wayfarer:** "What if we do not know which is which?"
**Pell:** "Then listen before you reach."
**Iri:** "I wish I had heard that sooner."
**Pell:** "You heard it when you were ready."

## Character page — The Verdant Colossus

The Colossus was once called the Root Listener.
It was not born as a warrior.
It was a guardian grown from a tree that had survived three seasons of forgetting.
The wardens asked it to hold the first note.
It agreed because the forest had already been keeping the paths open.
When the Quiet reached Mosslight, the Root Listener tried to preserve every warning.
It bound the bell into its chest.
The bell's sound became part of its body.
The guardian grew larger as the warning became heavier.
It learned to mistake stillness for safety.
It did not know that a warning must eventually be heard.
Its roots crossed the paths.
Its branches covered the doors.
The forest began to treat it as a wall.
The Colossus did not understand that it had become the obstacle.
When the Wayfarer approached, it tested whether they would strike first.
The Wayfarer listened.
The Colossus allowed the note to return.
Afterward it remained in Briar Well.
The mossling continued to sleep beside it.
The guardian's work changed from holding the warning to tending the garden that had grown around it.
It never became small.
It became gentle.

## Ending beat of Act I

The Wayfarer crossed the final bridge at dusk.
The forest opened behind them.
The bell flowers rang once.
Pell stood beneath the canopy.
The mossling lifted its head.
The thorn sprite circled the gate.
Iri looked back at the observatory.
She could see only a line of pale light.
The lantern held the first note.
It did not shine brighter than before.
It shone more evenly.
The Wayfarer asked whether the other notes would be found.
Iri answered that the notes were not lost.
They were waiting where people still practiced their meanings.
The road turned toward a red horizon.
A warm wind reached them.
It smelled of ash, bread, and rain on stone.
Iri smiled.
"Ember Hollow," she said.
The Wayfarer asked whether the next guardian would be a monster.
Iri answered, "It may look like one."
"Will it be one?"
"That depends on whether we remember to listen."

# Act III — The Library of Unsaid Names
## Frostglass Vale
### The person is not the record

**The third note: Name.**
**The third guardian: The Hollow Monarch.**
**The movement art awakened: Double-Jump.**

## Chapter 1 — The valley beneath the aurora

The road from Ember Hollow rose into blue air.
The warmth faded slowly.
The stone grew pale.
Snow fell without touching the ground.
It drifted through the valley like a thought that had not yet settled.
Above the mountains, an aurora unfolded in ribbons of white and lavender.
Frostglass Vale was beautiful in the way a held breath was beautiful.
Every bridge was clear.
Every tower was quiet.
Every window reflected a face that might have been standing somewhere else.
The Wayfarer stepped onto the first glass causeway.
Their reflection moved a fraction too late.
Iri stopped.
"Do not ask the ice what it remembers."
"Why?"
"It remembers everything that was said near it."
"That sounds useful."
"It also remembers what people were not ready to say."
The Wayfarer looked down.
The ice beneath their feet held a faint image of a city.
It was Moonwake.
The observatory stood bright.
People crossed the streets.
A figure walked beside Iri.
The face was blurred.
Iri looked away.
The Wayfarer did not ask.
A door opened in the cliff.
Beyond it, shelves of ice rose into the mountain.
The Name Library waited.
A voice spoke from somewhere inside.
"Please leave the names where you found them."
Iri answered, "We have not come to take them."
The door opened wider.
The voice said, "Everyone says that first."

## Chapter 2 — The Name Library

### Edda, keeper of shelves

Edda stood at a desk carved from blue ice.
Her coat was lined with soft wool.
Her hands were ink-stained.
A small silver chain held a key around her neck.
The key opened no door.
It was a reminder of a door she had chosen to keep closed.
Edda looked at the Wayfarer.
Then she looked at the lantern.
"That should not be here."
"It has travelled with us."
"It should not be here," she repeated.
Iri stepped forward.
"Edda."
The keeper's expression changed.
She knew Iri's voice.
She did not know whether she trusted it.
"You have returned."
"I think I have."
"That is not how returns work."
"Perhaps I am learning."
Edda turned toward the shelves.
"Names are kept in the inner stacks."
"Are they safe?"
"They are preserved."
"That is not the same thing."
Edda looked sharply at the Wayfarer.
"Who taught you to say that?"
The Wayfarer did not know.
The lantern warmed.
Edda opened a ledger.
Every page held a name.
Some names were written in silver.
Some were written in blue.
Some had been crossed out.
Some had been written again in a different hand.
A few pages were blank.
Edda touched a blank page.
"These are the most difficult."
"Why?"
"Because no one knows what they should say."
The Wayfarer asked whether a blank page meant someone had been forgotten.
Edda said, "Sometimes."
Then she corrected herself.
"Sometimes it means someone has chosen not to be recorded."
She placed the ledger on the desk.
"I have not always known the difference."
Iri looked at her.
"Neither have I."
The two stood in silence.
The library listened.

### The shelves of names

The Name Library was built in three descending levels.
The upper shelves held names that people had chosen to share.
The middle shelves held names entrusted to the archive by families and friends.
The lower shelves held names whose owners had not yet returned to decide what should happen to them.
The lowest room was sealed.
Edda said it contained the names that the Quiet had touched.
The Wayfarer asked whether those names could be recovered.
Edda said that some could.
Some could not.
Some no longer belonged to the person who had first carried them.
The Wayfarer asked how a name could stop belonging to its owner.
Edda answered, "It cannot."
Then she looked at the sealed door.
"I have behaved as though it could."
She opened the upper shelves.
The first name glowed.
It belonged to a person who had asked to be remembered as a gardener.
The next name belonged to someone who had left the valley.
The third belonged to someone who had changed it twice.
Edda had recorded every version.
She had also written the date on which each version was chosen.
The Wayfarer asked why.
"So no one could claim the earlier one was the true one."
"Was that a problem?"
"It became one."
Edda closed the book.
"People began to ask which name was the original."
"Which one was?"
Edda looked toward the blank page.
"The one the person chose to carry now."

### A quiet room for changing names

The room was small.
It contained a mirror, a chair, and a bowl of water.
No one was allowed to record what was spoken there.
A person could sit.
They could speak a name.
They could leave without speaking.
They could ask for a new name.
They could ask for the old one to be removed from the public shelves.
The room had been built after the first frost.
The Hollow Monarch had once praised it.
Later, the Monarch had ordered it sealed.
Edda kept the key.
She had not used it.
The Wayfarer asked whether the room could be opened again.
Edda said that doing so would mean admitting the library had been wrong.
Iri answered, "A library is not a person."
Edda looked at her.
"A library can still hurt people."
"Yes."
"Then it can still change."
The Wayfarer waited.
Edda took the key from her neck.
She opened the room.
A small warmth passed through the ice.
The mirror reflected no face.
It reflected the chair.
That was enough.

## Chapter 3 — The name without ink

### The keeper's lost name

Edda knew every shelf.
She knew the order of every letter.
She knew the oldest names by sound.
She could find a page by remembering the rhythm of its first syllable.
She could not remember her own name.
The Quiet had taken it while she was copying the names of the valley.
At first, she had asked the archive to find it.
The archive found a dozen possibilities.
She rejected each one.
Some belonged to people she had loved.
Some belonged to people she had never met.
Some belonged to a person she might once have been.
She decided that she would wait until the true name appeared.
Years passed.
The name never returned.
She began to believe that she had no right to choose a different one.
The Wayfarer asked whether a name had to come from the past.
Edda looked at the floor.
"That is what the Monarch taught us."
"Did the Monarch ask you to believe it?"
"It did not have to."
"Why not?"
"Because I was afraid."
The Wayfarer asked what she was afraid of losing.
Edda answered, "The proof that I had ever been here."
Iri said, "You are here now."
Edda closed her eyes.
The sentence made her angry.
Then it made her tired.
Then it made her hopeful.
She took the ledger from the desk.
She opened to the page that had been blank for centuries.
She wrote one word.
Edda.
She did not know whether it had been her first name.
She knew that it was the name she chose to use.
The ink did not freeze.
The paper warmed.
The library made room.

### Quest — The Name Without Ink

The Wayfarer returned to Edda after exploring the name shelves.
The keeper held two pages.
One contained the name she could not remember.
The other contained the name she had chosen.
She asked which page should be kept.
The Wayfarer asked whether she wanted both.
Edda said she did not know.
She feared that keeping the old name would make her feel trapped.
She feared that destroying it would make her feel erased.
The Wayfarer did not tell her what to do.
They offered two paths.
Speak Edda's name with her.
Or let Edda choose when to remember.
The Wayfarer chose to let Edda choose when to remember.
The keeper folded the old page.
She placed it in the private room.
The page was not destroyed.
It was not placed on public display.
It could remain until Edda wished to open it.
The new page stayed on the desk.
It held the name she carried now.
Edda said, "I thought a name was a door that only opened one way."
The Wayfarer asked what it was now.
"A room with more than one entrance."
The keeper smiled.
The third companion thread was remembered.
The library's lower shelves began to thaw.

### The voices beneath the ice

At night, the library spoke.
The voices were not ghosts.
They were memories preserved in the ice.
A child asked whether their name could be longer.
A traveller asked whether a name could be carried across a border.
An old woman asked whether the name she used for herself could be different from the one her family used.
A person who had left the valley asked whether the archive would keep their old name private.
The ice answered each question with a small movement.
The Wayfarer listened.
Edda stood beside them.
She did not write anything down.
The voices grew calmer.
One voice asked whether a person could be remembered without their name.
The Wayfarer looked at Iri.
Iri said, "Yes."
A second voice asked whether a name could be remembered without the person.
Edda answered, "Only as a record."
The voice asked whether a record was a person.
Edda said, "No."
The library grew warmer.
A crack appeared in the sealed door.
The Hollow Monarch had heard the answers.
It began to wake.

## Chapter 4 — Glass Causeway

### The bridge that changed

The Glass Causeway crossed a frozen river.
Its surface was clear.
Beneath it, currents moved in slow circles.
The bridge had once been a wall around the Name Library.
The first keeper of the vale had built it to protect the shelves.
When the river rose, the wall became a bridge.
The people began to cross it.
The keeper tried to restore the wall.
The river rose again.
The bridge returned.
The people called it a lesson.
The Monarch called it a failure.
A glacier warden stood near the centre.
Its shield was clear as a winter morning.
It raised the shield before sending a low wave of ice across the bridge.
The Wayfarer watched the warning.
They climbed onto a high platform.
The wave passed beneath them.
The guardian lowered its shield.
The Wayfarer struck once.
The shield cracked.
The guardian stepped back.
It did not attack again.
The Wayfarer asked why.
The creature pointed toward the bridge.
A small crack had formed beneath a support.
The Wayfarer repaired the support with a piece of iron from Ember Hollow.
The glacier warden lowered its head.
The bridge steadied.
The creature moved away.
The Wayfarer had not defeated it.
They had answered its concern.
The bridge led to the White Mirror.
The second hidden memory of the act waited there.

### Secret — The White Mirror

The White Mirror stood in a narrow room.
It had no frame.
It was made from one sheet of clear ice.
The Wayfarer approached.
Their reflection appeared.
Then the reflection changed.
It showed Edda with a name written above her head.
The name was not Edda.
The letters began to melt.
Edda entered the room.
She looked at the mirror.
For a moment, she seemed to recognise the name.
Then she looked away.
The Wayfarer asked whether the mirror had shown her true name.
Edda answered, "It showed a name I once carried."
"Do you want it back?"
"I want to know that I could have carried it."
The ice softened.
A small shard fell into the Wayfarer's hand.
The secret mark appeared.
It did not reveal the name.
It revealed the choice.
Edda had once asked the library to remember her.
Later, she had asked it to stop speaking for her.
The mirror kept both requests.
The Wayfarer placed the shard in the lantern.
The glass gained a thin blue line.
The room became quiet.
Edda said, "Thank you for not asking what it said."
The Wayfarer nodded.
The mirror remained blank.

## Chapter 5 — The Hushed Throne

### The Monarch of held names

The Hushed Throne stood at the end of the vale.
It was built from white ice and dark glass.
Its halls were lined with frozen names.
Every name had been preserved perfectly.
No letter had melted.
No voice had changed.
No person had been allowed to grow beyond the page.
The Hollow Monarch stood beneath the highest arch.
Its crown was made from frozen script.
Its cloak carried the names of entire families.
Its hands were clear as glass.
It watched the Wayfarer enter.
"You have brought the notes."
"We have carried them."
"That is not the same."
"No."
The Monarch looked at Edda.
"You opened the private room."
"Yes."
"You let a name remain unwritten."
"Yes."
"You allowed a person to change."
"Yes."
The Monarch raised its hands.
Two crescents of ice moved across the floor.
The Wayfarer jumped.
A pause followed.
Then a star of ice fell from above.
The attack came in a pattern.
The Wayfarer learned the pattern.
They moved between the marks.
They struck when the Monarch paused.
The first strike cracked the crown.
The second made the frozen names tremble.
The Monarch awakened.
Its voice grew louder.
"You will lose them."
"Some things are lost."
"Then you will have nothing."
"We will still have one another."
The Monarch sent two more crescents.
The Wayfarer crossed the arena.
The ice beneath their feet broke.
They jumped again.
The double-jump art had not yet awakened.
They fell toward the lower platform.
The lantern flashed.
A pale blue line appeared beneath them.
The Wayfarer landed.
The Monarch lowered its head.
The battle continued.
The Wayfarer used the lantern strike.
The frozen pages opened.
Names escaped as small lights.
They did not fly toward the Monarch.
They flew toward the library.
The Monarch watched.
For the first time, it looked afraid.

### The frozen choir

The choir began as a sound beneath the floor.
It was made from the names that had been held too long.
Some voices were angry.
Some were gentle.
Some had forgotten the words they wished to say.
The Monarch tried to silence them.
Edda stepped forward.
She spoke her chosen name.
The choir answered.
She said, "You belong to yourselves."
The voices grew stronger.
The Monarch's crown began to melt.
The Wayfarer did not strike.
They waited.
The Monarch lowered its hands.
The final note of ice fell from the crown.
It landed in the centre of the chamber.
The third note returned.
Name.
It entered the lantern.
The guardian's face changed.
Beneath the crown was a person who had once been called the Keeper of Names.
The Monarch asked whether the archive would forget it.
Edda answered, "The archive will remember what you choose to leave."
The Monarch said, "I do not know what that is."
Edda replied, "Then you may begin with nothing."
The guardian looked at the blank page.
It began to cry.
The tears did not freeze.
They fell into the clear pool beneath the throne.
The pool reflected the aurora.
The names were released to their owners.
Some returned to the shelves.
Some disappeared into the valley.
Some remained unspoken.
The library no longer treated silence as a failure.
The third note rested beside the first two.
The double-jump awakened.
The Wayfarer felt the air hold them for a brief second.
They leapt once.
Then they leapt again.
The higher platform was no longer unreachable.
The wall had become a path through the air.

## Chapter 6 — The name that changes

### Edda's page

After the Monarch's release, Edda returned to the private room.
She placed the old page in a small drawer.
She placed the new page on the desk.
The drawer was unlocked.
The desk was in the open.
She did not throw away the old name.
She did not make the new name permanent.
She said that she might change it again.
The Wayfarer asked whether that would trouble the archive.
Edda smiled.
"The archive will learn."
She removed the silver chain from her neck.
The key no longer had to remind her of a closed door.
She placed it beside the blank page.
The key could open the private room.
The room could remain open.
The library had changed its law.
No record would be shown without permission.
No person would be required to keep an old name.
No person would be forced to choose a new one.
A name could be held.
A name could be released.
A name could be changed.
The person remained more than the record.

### The Monarch's apology

The former Monarch sat beside the pool.
It had no crown.
Its hands were bare.
The Wayfarer sat at a respectful distance.
The Monarch said that it had believed preservation was kindness.
It had been afraid that every change would become a loss.
It had frozen the names to keep the Quiet from reaching them.
It had also frozen the people who carried them.
The Wayfarer asked whether the Monarch regretted it.
The guardian said yes.
It also said that regret did not restore the years.
Edda joined them.
She did not forgive the Monarch immediately.
She told it that they could begin with the shelves.
The Monarch asked what to do.
Edda said, "Open the doors."
The Monarch rose.
Together, they opened the lower stacks.
No fanfare followed.
No name returned all at once.
The work would take time.
The Monarch accepted that.
It was the first time it had accepted a task with no final measure.
The Wayfarer left the room.
Iri remained behind.
She sat beside Edda.
The lantern glowed between them.
Neither spoke.
The silence was no longer a punishment.

### The road to the Astral Archive

The valley's aurora grew brighter.
A star appeared above the Hushed Throne.
It moved from one part of the sky to another.
Nym's map had begun to change.
The Wayfarer looked toward the distant observatories.
Iri said that the next kingdom would be difficult to follow.
"Why?"
"Because the stars do not always agree with the map."
"Which one should we trust?"
"Both, when they are useful."
"And when they are not?"
"Then we ask where the people are."
The Wayfarer touched the three notes.
Return held the path.
Hearth held the warmth.
Name held the choice.
The lantern's light was steadier.
The road opened across the snow.
Frostglass did not close behind them.
The names had been given back to their owners.
The valley was learning to speak in the present tense.
The Wayfarer and Iri climbed toward the stars.

## The Frostglass Chronicle — Recovered pages

### Glacier Mouth

The valley began at a blue stone opening.
The walls were smooth from centuries of wind.
A small inscription read: "Enter as you are."
The inscription had been carved by someone who did not wish to be named.
The snow had never covered it.
The glacier kept the words visible without keeping the hand that carved them.

### Blueice Causeway

The causeway had been built from transparent ice.
It reflected the sky beneath the travellers.
Its surface changed slowly with the season.
A bridge that never changed would have become a wall.
The vale had learned to let its roads move.

### The Frozen Choir

The choir was a chamber of names spoken softly.
No singer stood at the centre.
The voices moved through the ice.
They were not required to sing together.
Each voice had its own pace.
The choir was beautiful because no one was forced into harmony.

### Snowblind Halls

The halls were built to shelter travellers from white storms.
Every corner held a bench.
Every bench held a small lamp.
The lamps were turned low so that a tired person could sleep.
No one was asked to explain why they had stopped.

### Crystal Burrows

The burrows were tunnels cut by water through the ice.
They connected rooms that had once been separate.
The tunnels changed after every thaw.
The people of Frostglass stopped trying to keep a permanent map.
They began to leave signs that could be moved.

### The Rime Library

The library held books written in frost.
The words appeared only when the reader breathed gently.
A hurried reader saw only white pages.
The library taught patience without saying that patience was a virtue.
It simply waited.

### Whitefang Pass

The pass was named for a pale ridge that resembled a tooth.
It was a dangerous route.
The guides marked safe places with small blue stones.
The stones were never removed after a journey.
A future traveller might need them.

### The Shiverdeep

The Shiverdeep was a cavern that held the sound of winter.
It did not contain the coldest ice.
It contained the longest echo.
Travellers heard their own voices return from distant walls.
Some heard a word they had forgotten.
Some heard nothing.
Both were allowed.

### Mirrorfrost Gallery

The gallery was filled with mirrors that did not reflect the present.
Each mirror showed a choice.
The mirrors did not reveal what would have happened.
They revealed what the person had feared, hoped, or wondered.
Visitors were asked not to treat the reflections as instructions.

### The Pale Reservoir

The reservoir held water from the first thaw.
The water was clear.
It carried small pieces of ice that moved like stars.
The keeper of the reservoir never drank from it.
The water belonged to the valley.

### Winterglass Vault

The vault held names entrusted to the archive.
Its doors had no locks.
They opened only when the keeper asked permission to enter.
The names inside were not secrets.
They were private.

### The Silent Keep

The keep was built as a shelter.
It later became the Monarch's fortress.
After the guardian's release, the people turned it into a school.
Its first lesson was that a record is not a person.

### Frostworn Chapel

The chapel had no altar.
It had a circle of mirrors.
Visitors could sit and speak a name.
They could also sit without speaking.
The room did not distinguish between the two.

### The Boreal Maze

The maze moved with the aurora.
It was impossible to map completely.
Nym once tried.
The map grew too large to carry.
The Wayfarer crossed it by following the sound of people calling to one another.

### The Stillness Ring

The ring was the chamber of the Hollow Monarch.
Its floor held the outlines of names.
The lines were beginning to soften.
The ice was learning that release could be gentle.

### The Heart of Winter

The Heart of Winter was the pool beneath the throne.
It was not frozen.
It held the third note.
The water reflected every face without asking for a name.
The vale called it the place where a person could begin again.

## Character page — The Hollow Monarch

The Hollow Monarch was once the Keeper of Names.
It had been a careful archivist.
It believed that a name should never be lost.
It had watched people forget one another during the first winter of the Quiet.
It had seen a family search for a name that no longer answered.
It had seen a traveller arrive with a new name and be told that the old one was the only true one.
The keeper tried to protect the names.
It froze them.
At first, the ice was a shelter.
Later, it became a cage.
The keeper began to believe that a living person was less reliable than a perfect record.
It stopped asking permission.
It stopped opening the private room.
It stopped allowing the names to change.
The shelves filled.
The people grew silent.
The keeper became the Hollow Monarch.
Its crown was made from the names it could not release.
Its throne was a promise that had forgotten its purpose.
The Wayfarer did not defeat the Monarch by destroying the archive.
They defeated it by returning choice to the people.
The Monarch's crown melted.
The notes were released.
The keeper became a listener again.
It continued to repair the library.
It did not ask to be forgiven as a condition of that work.
It accepted that the people could decide for themselves.

### The Monarch speaks

"I kept the names because I feared the silence."
"I made the shelves taller."
"I made the doors heavier."
"I made the records perfect."
"I forgot that a person could change."
"I forgot that a name was not a chain."
"I forgot that permission could be withdrawn."
"I called the ice a shelter."
"I did not notice when it became a wall."
"Edda opened the private room."
"The voices beneath the floor were not ghosts."
"They were people asking to be allowed to speak."
"I cannot return every year I kept them still."
"I can open the shelves."
"I can leave the pages blank."
"I can learn to ask."

## Ending beat of Act III

The Wayfarer climbed the ridge beyond Frostglass.
The aurora moved above them.
The third note glowed blue in the lantern.
Edda stood at the library door.
She held two pages.
One was private.
One was open.
She waved.
The Wayfarer waved back.
Iri watched the valley.
"She chose a name."
"Yes."
"Will she keep it?"
"That is her choice."
The Wayfarer smiled.
The road ahead crossed the midnight sea.
Beyond it, observatories turned around one another.
A map hung in the sky.
One room remained blank.
The Wayfarer asked whether the blank room was a mistake.
Iri answered, "No."
"What is it?"
"A promise that the story is not finished."

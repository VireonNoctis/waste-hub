# Act II — The Hearth That Kept the Dark
## Ember Hollow
### A city that chose to remember for one another

**The second note: Hearth.**
**The second guardian: The Ember Sovereign.**
**The movement art awakened: Wall-Jump.**

## Chapter 1 — A city inside a cooled star

The road from Mosslight did not end at a gate.
It descended through black stone and opened into a wide caldera.
Ember Hollow lay inside the old heart of a mountain.
Its roofs were dark glass.
Its windows glowed like small amber moons.
Thin bridges crossed the empty places between towers.
Below them, rivers of cooled slag held the memory of fire.
There was warmth in the air, but no smoke.
The city had learned to keep its furnaces low.
Its people called that practice the Quiet Flame.
They did not mean the Quiet that gathered around forgotten names.
They meant a hearth that could warm a room without consuming it.
At every street corner stood a public fire.
Some fires were barely larger than candles.
Some warmed whole courtyards.
Each had a ring of stones around it.
The stones were marked by the names of people who had tended them.
No stone was larger than another.
A person could add a mark.
A person could also ask for a mark to be removed.
The Wayfarer watched a child place a small piece of charcoal beside a fire.
The child did not write a name.
They drew a circle.
The circle was open at one side.
Iri recognised it.
"That is a place kept free."
"For whom?"
"For whoever needs it."
The Wayfarer looked at the many open circles around the city.
Some were old.
Some were new.
One held a little brass cup.
Another held a scarf.
A third held nothing.
A warm wind passed through the street.
The empty circle flickered.
It did not close.
Ember Hollow had made a custom of leaving room.
It had also forgotten why the custom mattered.
The furnaces were growing brighter.
The public fires were beginning to dim.
The city needed someone to remember the difference between warmth and burning.

## Chapter 2 — Cinder Market

### The market of small offerings

Cinder Market was crowded with ordinary wonders.
A seller offered glass beads filled with captured dawn.
A baker sold bread shaped like little moons.
A tailor repaired coats with thread spun from ash.
A potter made cups that warmed only when held by two people.
No one sold memories.
The city had once tried.
It had stopped when a memory was mistaken for something that could be owned.
The market had kept one stall empty since that day.
A sign above it read: "For the guest who has not arrived."
Vey stood behind the stall beside a small hearth.
He wore a coat marked by pale burn scars.
His hands were blackened with soot.
He was polishing a cup that had never been used.
The Wayfarer approached.
Vey looked at the lantern.
"That is a long road to carry a little light."
"It does not burn."
"Then perhaps it is the safer sort."
Iri stepped forward.
"Vey."
He looked at her for a long time.
"I know the shape of your voice."
"Do you remember my name?"
"No."
"That is all right."
Vey placed the cup on the table.
"I kept a place for a name I could not pronounce."
The Wayfarer asked who it belonged to.
Vey shook his head.
"That is the promise."
He tapped the empty stall.
"I was asked not to decide who would come."
"Who asked you?"
"I do not know."
"Then why keep the promise?"
Vey smiled.
"Because I remember making it freely."
The Wayfarer looked at the hearth.
Its flame was small.
It was warm enough for three people.
Vey offered them bread.
Iri accepted.
The Wayfarer accepted.
Vey sat down.
For a moment, the three of them were simply people sharing food.
The lantern glowed.
The city seemed to breathe more easily.
Then a furnace bell rang.
The market windows turned orange.
Vey stood at once.
"Something has taken the heat."
He looked toward the Last Hearth.
"It is happening again."

### The Ash-smith's trade

Vey was the last ash-smith.
He did not forge weapons for the city.
He repaired the tools that kept its public fires safe.
He made shutters for furnaces.
He made iron rings for hearth stones.
He made small bells that could be heard without frightening anyone.
His greatest work was the Quiet Flame.
It was a method of heating rooms without feeding the furnace more than it needed.
The method depended on sharing.
A person tended the fire for a short time.
Another person took their place.
A third person checked that the room was not too warm.
No one stayed until they were exhausted.
The old city had called this inefficient.
The people of Ember Hollow had kept it anyway.
Vey told the Wayfarer that his teacher had taught him the method.
The teacher's face was gone from his memory.
The teacher's hands remained.
They had been broad.
They had carried a small burn across one thumb.
They had always warmed the tools before passing them to someone else.
Vey remembered one sentence.
"Never make the next person touch what you could have made gentle."
The Wayfarer asked whether he had written it down.
Vey shook his head.
"I did not want the sentence to become a rule."
"Why not?"
"Because the teacher said it to me."
"He did not ask you to preserve it?"
"He asked me to use it."
Vey placed a warm cup in the Wayfarer's hands.
The cup did not burn.
It was made for the next person.
Vey's promise was like that.
It was not a monument.
It was a practice.

### Quest — A Hearth for Two

Vey's small hearth had two places beside it.
One was used every evening.
The other remained open.
The city had asked him to close the empty place.
They believed it was wasteful to keep a seat for someone who might never arrive.
Vey refused.
He did not know whether the guest would be a person, a family, or a stranger who needed a night of warmth.
He only remembered the request.
The Wayfarer listened to the story.
Iri looked at the empty place.
She asked whether Vey wanted to keep it forever.
Vey said that he wanted the promise to remain kind.
A promise could become cruel when it refused to change.
He asked the Wayfarer to choose what should happen.
"Keep the hearth for the unknown guest," Vey said.
"Or give the ember back to the city."
The Wayfarer could not choose for Vey.
They asked what he wanted.
Vey looked at the flame.
"I want the city to understand that a place is not wasted because it is open."
The Wayfarer chose to keep the hearth open.
Vey did not thank them.
He invited them to sit.
The three ate together.
The empty place remained.
Later, Vey placed a small amber chip in the lantern's path.
It held the second hidden memory.
The open seat had never belonged to one forgotten guest.
It had been reserved for the next person who arrived without a name.
Vey's promise was made to the world, not to a single face.
The Wayfarer understood that the promise could survive without knowing who would need it.
The second companion thread was remembered.
The public fire grew steady.

## Chapter 3 — Slag Bridge

### The bridge above sleeping fire

Slag Bridge crossed the deepest part of the caldera.
It was held by chains and old stone.
The bridge moved slightly when the mountain breathed.
Below it, red light glowed through cracks.
The city called the light sleeping fire.
It was not safe to fall.
It was also not safe to rush.
A small ash hound patrolled the bridge.
It left a trail of warm cinders.
It lowered its head before each lunge.
The Wayfarer watched the pattern.
The hound's eyes were pale.
Its paws did not leave marks.
It was not hunting for food.
It was guarding a broken chain.
The chain had once held the bridge in place.
A traveller had tried to force the bridge open years ago.
The hound had remained beside the damage ever since.
The Wayfarer moved carefully.
The hound lunged.
The Wayfarer stepped aside.
The hound stopped.
It looked at the broken chain.
Iri knelt beside it.
"You have been here a long time."
The hound's ears lowered.
The Wayfarer used the lantern to warm the chain.
The metal brightened.
The hound moved away.
The bridge settled into place.
The hound lay down.
It had not needed to be defeated.
It had needed the bridge to be safe again.
The Wayfarer left it a piece of bread.
The hound did not eat immediately.
It waited until they had crossed.
Then it carried the bread beneath the bridge.
The city did not count the act as a victory.
The hound did not count it as a defeat.
The bridge remained open.

### The glass kiln

The hidden kiln lay beneath a broken arch.
Its door was covered in black glass.
The Wayfarer found it by following the amber chip.
The lantern's reflection made a second door appear.
Inside, the kiln was cold.
A row of small chairs stood beside the furnace.
Each chair had a cup.
The cups were empty.
The chairs were different sizes.
One was made for a child.
One was made for someone with a cane.
One was made for a person who preferred to sit near the door.
The fourth was a simple stool.
A sign read: "No guest must explain their arrival."
The Wayfarer touched the stool.
A memory opened.
Vey stood beside a younger Orin.
Orin was tired.
Their name had already begun to fade.
Vey offered a cup.
Orin declined.
Vey did not insist.
He simply left the cup within reach.
Orin returned later.
The cup was still warm.
Auralis stood in the doorway.
She did not record the moment.
She watched it.
The memory ended with a chair being placed beside the hearth.
The second secret mark appeared.
It was not a key to a treasure.
It was proof that the promise had been made before the Quiet reached the city.
The Wayfarer carried it away.
The kiln remained cold.
It had completed its work.

### The bridge's small history

The bridge had been built by three crews.
The first crew laid the stone.
The second crew made the chains.
The third crew repaired the damage after the mountain shook.
Each crew disagreed with the others.
The first wanted the bridge to be permanent.
The second wanted it to be easy to repair.
The third wanted it to be safe even when it was broken.
The final design carried all three ideas.
The bridge could move.
The bridge could be repaired.
The bridge could be closed without collapsing.
A plaque listed the crews.
Beside the names was a blank space.
The Wayfarer asked why.
A nearby worker explained that the blank was for people who would maintain the bridge in the future.
The worker had never met those people.
They had still built a place for them.
The Wayfarer understood another meaning of Hearth.
It was not only warmth.
It was the work that made warmth possible tomorrow.
A public fire required someone to tend it.
A bridge required someone to inspect it.
A promise required someone to notice when it became too heavy.
The city had survived because people continued to do ordinary work.
The Quiet had not erased that work.
It had made it difficult to remember who was sharing it.

## Chapter 4 — The Last Hearth

### The furnace that would not rest

The Last Hearth stood in the deepest chamber of Ember Hollow.
It was a public furnace built around a pale ember.
The Ember Sovereign stood before it.
Its body was shaped from black iron and warm glass.
Its crown was a ring of small chimneys.
Light moved through its chest.
The Wayfarer saw that the light was not flame.
It was memory.
The Sovereign had been feeding the furnace with its own past.
It had given away its childhood.
It had given away the name of its first friend.
It had given away the sound of a door opening.
It had given away the reason it had become a guardian.
Each memory had warmed one district.
The city had mistaken the brightness for strength.
The Sovereign had mistaken exhaustion for duty.
The Wayfarer approached.
The Sovereign raised three ember marks.
They appeared on the floor.
The first marked a safe place.
The second marked a place that would soon become dangerous.
The third marked the path of a wide sweep.
The Wayfarer learned the pattern.
They waited.
They moved.
They struck only when the Sovereign lowered its guard.
The first blow made the chimneys ring.
The second showed a small chair inside its chest.
The third made the furnace dim.
The Sovereign awakened.
Its crown burned brighter.
The room filled with heat.
The Wayfarer used the first note of Return.
The green light crossed the floor.
The Sovereign paused.
It recognised the sound of an open path.
The battle continued.
The Wayfarer did not try to destroy the furnace.
They tried to show the Sovereign that the fire could be shared.
The Sovereign lowered its arms.
The light inside its chest became small.
It spoke.
"I have kept the hearth burning."
"Who asked you to?"
"I cannot remember."
"Then why have you kept going?"
"Because the people were cold."
"Are they still cold?"
The Sovereign looked toward the city.
The public fires were dim.
The workers were waiting.
The Wayfarer asked whether the city could help.
The Sovereign did not answer.
It had forgotten how to ask.

### The second note

Iri placed the lantern beside the furnace.
Vey entered the chamber carrying a small iron ring.
He had made it for the Quiet Flame.
It could divide heat across several hearths.
The Sovereign watched him.
Vey did not bow.
He placed the ring around the pale ember.
The ring warmed.
The furnace's light moved into the public channels.
It did not grow brighter.
It became more even.
The city lights steadied.
The Sovereign lowered its crown.
A voice came from the furnace.
It was the sound of an old promise.
The second note returned.
Hearth.
The note entered the lantern.
The Sovereign's body cooled.
Its face became less severe.
It remembered its name had once been spoken by a friend.
It did not remember the name itself.
It did not need to.
It remembered the laughter that followed it.
Vey sat beside the Sovereign.
The two watched the fire.
The Sovereign said, "I have nothing left to give."
Vey answered, "Then take your turn resting."
The Sovereign asked who would tend the fire.
Vey pointed toward the open door.
"Everyone who chooses."
The Sovereign bowed its head.
The guardian was not destroyed.
It was released from a duty no one had asked it to carry alone.
The second movement art awakened.
The Wayfarer felt the wall beside them become a path.
The mark of Wall-Jump appeared.
The ability was the art of finding a foothold where a wall had once seemed final.
It did not break the wall.
It changed the relationship between the traveller and the wall.
The Wayfarer leapt from stone to stone.
They reached a high ledge.
The mountain no longer felt like a prison.
It felt like a place with many ways through.

## Chapter 5 — A place for the next guest

### Vey's promise

After the Sovereign's release, Ember Hollow gathered around the public fires.
No grand ceremony was held.
The city had no reason to celebrate one guardian's suffering.
Instead, the people made a schedule.
Every hearth would have several keepers.
No keeper would be required to serve beyond their strength.
A person could step away without explaining why.
A person could return when they were ready.
The empty circles remained.
Some were filled with cups.
Some were filled with small stones.
One was filled with a paper star.
The open place beside Vey's hearth remained empty.
The Wayfarer asked whether the unknown guest had arrived.
Vey smiled.
"Perhaps."
"Who?"
"All of you."
The Wayfarer sat.
Iri sat.
Vey sat.
A fourth cup was placed nearby.
No one claimed it.
A child arrived and took it.
The child had been cold.
The chair had been waiting for them.
Vey did not ask their name.
The child offered it anyway.
The name was Sella.
Vey repeated it carefully.
The child smiled.
The hearth grew warm.
That evening, Vey returned the small cup to the stall.
The cup no longer needed to wait.
The promise had become a practice.
It could continue without him.

### Iri and the forgotten face

Iri stood by the furnace after the crowd had gone.
She watched the Quiet Flame move through the channels.
The light reminded her of a memory.
She remembered a person sitting beside an open window.
She remembered the sound of a cup placed on wood.
She remembered being asked whether a lantern could be a home.
She had answered that a lantern was a way to find one.
The face of the speaker remained hidden.
Iri did not force it to return.
She told the Wayfarer what she remembered.
The Wayfarer asked whether the person had been kind.
Iri said, "They left the cup within reach."
"That sounds kind."
"Yes."
"Do you need to know their face?"
Iri thought about it.
"No."
The lantern glowed.
The second note rested beside the first.
Iri began to hum.
The melody had no words.
It was enough for now.

### The road to Frostglass

The road from Ember Hollow rose along the caldera wall.
The air grew cold.
A pale blue light appeared beyond the stone.
Iri wrapped her wings close.
The Wayfarer felt the lantern cool.
Ahead lay a valley of glass and snow.
The city of names waited beneath the aurora.
Iri said that the next guardian feared change.
The Wayfarer asked whether that made the guardian wrong.
Iri answered, "It made the guardian afraid."
"Can fear be dangerous?"
"Yes."
"Can it also be understandable?"
"Yes."
The Wayfarer looked at the path.
The second note warmed the lantern.
The first note held the way behind them.
The road ahead was not easier.
It was open.
They entered Frostglass Vale.

## The Ember Hollow Chronicle — Recovered pages

### Cinderfall Entry

The entry road descends through a chimney of black stone.
Every wall bears the marks of old hands.
Some marks show where a tool was lifted.
Some show where a person stopped to rest.
The city preserved the resting places as carefully as the workshops.
A traveller could find a seat every few hundred steps.
The seats were heated by warm stone.
No one was asked to pay.

### Ashen Barracks

The barracks were once used by the furnace guards.
They became a place where workers could sleep between shifts.
The old armour stands were turned into coat hooks.
The weapons were melted into hinges.
The room kept one bell.
It rang when a worker had been awake too long.
The bell did not summon a supervisor.
It summoned a friend.

### The Coalworks

The Coalworks no longer burned coal.
It refined warmth from the deep stone.
Its machines were designed to stop when unattended.
The first machine had once run for twenty days.
The worker who built it had collapsed beside the furnace.
The city remembered that failure.
Every machine now had a rest cycle.
The rest cycle was part of the design.

### Sootglass Gallery

The gallery held windows made from cooled ash.
Each pane showed a different colour when the moon crossed the mountain.
The panes were not arranged by beauty.
They were arranged by the people who had repaired them.
A broken pane was never hidden.
It was marked with a small silver thread.
The thread reminded visitors that repair was part of the gallery.

### Magma Reliquary

The reliquary held the first tools of the ash-smiths.
None had been polished.
The tools were displayed with their worn handles.
The city believed that a tool should be remembered by the work it had done.
A hammer with a smooth handle had helped many people.
A hammer with a chipped head had taught someone to slow down.

### The Furnace Run

The run was a wide channel beneath the city.
Warmth moved through it toward the public fires.
The channel had small doors every few steps.
A worker could close one section without stopping the whole system.
The doors were called the mercy gates.
They allowed a repair to happen without making the rest of the city cold.

### Scoria Bastion

The bastion was built around a cracked pillar.
The pillar had once held the ceiling.
It was no longer strong enough to carry the weight.
The workers built new supports around it.
They did not remove the pillar.
It remained as a record of what had once been carried.
The bastion taught that strength could include asking for help.

### The Smelter Depths

The smelters worked in pairs.
One person watched the temperature.
Another watched the people.
The city had learned that a safe furnace required both kinds of attention.
A machine could be measured.
A tired worker had to be heard.

### Charred Throneway

The road led to an old throne room.
The throne had been removed.
A long table stood in its place.
The seats were all different.
Some were high.
Some were low.
One had wheels.
The table was built so that no single chair could stand at its head.

### The Obsidian Kiln

The kiln once made glass for the observatory.
Its old plans were lost.
Its tools survived.
The glassworkers continued to make windows.
They left a faint amber line in every pane.
The line showed where the glass had cooled slowly.
It made the windows stronger.

### Embervault

The vault held letters that had been written but never sent.
Some were apologies.
Some were invitations.
Some were ordinary notes about bread, weather, and borrowed tools.
The letters were not opened.
Their writers had chosen to leave them private.
The vault protected the choice, not the contents.

### The Black Foundry

The foundry made the iron rings used by the Quiet Flame.
The rings were not all the same size.
Each was made to fit a particular hearth.
A worker measured the stones before shaping the metal.
The rings were built to serve the fire.
The fire was never expected to serve the ring.

### Pyreheart Tunnels

The tunnels carried warm air toward the outer districts.
A small ash hound lived near one bend.
It guarded a broken vent.
The vent was repaired before anyone attempted to move the hound.
Afterward, the hound followed the workers for three days.
It never asked to be given a name.

### The Crown of Ash

The crown was a ring of chimneys worn by the Ember Sovereign.
Each chimney represented a district.
The crown was not meant to show rank.
It was meant to show responsibility.
When one chimney dimmed, the others were supposed to help.
The Sovereign forgot this.
The Wayfarer reminded it.

### The Crucible Ring

The ring was the final chamber before the Last Hearth.
It contained the channels that divided heat.
The old system had been designed to let the Sovereign control every flame.
The new system allowed each district to tend its own fire.
The ring became a symbol of shared work.

### The Last Hearth

The Last Hearth was not the oldest fire.
It was the last one that had remained under a single keeper.
Its ember was small.
Its warmth was enough.
When the Sovereign released it, the furnace did not become brighter.
The city became warmer.

## Character page — The Ember Sovereign

The Ember Sovereign was once the Hearthkeeper.
It was chosen because it knew the difference between a flame and a furnace.
It believed that a hearth should make room for the cold.
It believed that warmth had to be shared to remain gentle.
When the Quiet began to gather, the public fires dimmed.
The Hearthkeeper gave one memory to each district.
The first memory warmed the western streets.
The second warmed the market.
The third warmed the bridges.
The fourth warmed the barracks.
The city praised the guardian.
The guardian believed that praise meant the sacrifice was working.
It gave away more.
Soon, it could remember the heat but not the hands that had taught it to tend a fire.
It became the Ember Sovereign.
Its crown grew heavier.
Its furnace grew hotter.
It stood at the Last Hearth and never sat down.
The people thought it was protecting them.
The guardian thought it was keeping a promise.
Both had forgotten the promise's condition.
No one was supposed to carry the fire alone.
When the Wayfarer arrived, the Sovereign expected another demand.
Instead, they brought the first note.
Return.
The first note reminded the Sovereign that a door could remain open.
Vey brought the iron ring.
The ring reminded it that warmth could travel.
The second note returned.
The Sovereign learned to rest.
It remained a guardian, but its work became shared.
Its crown became a set of open chimneys.
Its throne became a bench beside the public fire.

## Ending beat of Act II

The Wayfarer stood on the high bridge above the caldera.
The city lights glowed in separate windows.
No single furnace held them all.
Vey waved from the market.
The Sovereign sat beside the Last Hearth.
Iri carried the lantern.
The green and amber notes rested together.
A cold wind descended from the north.
The lantern's glass frosted at the edges.
Ahead, a blue-white aurora crossed the mountains.
Iri looked at the light.
"Frostglass Vale."
The Wayfarer asked whether the names there were safe.
Iri answered, "They have been kept very carefully."
"Is that good?"
"It depends on whether the people are allowed to change."
The road climbed.
The warmth of Ember Hollow remained behind them.
It did not vanish.
It had become a hundred small lights.
